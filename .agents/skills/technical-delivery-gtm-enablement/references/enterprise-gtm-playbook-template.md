## Buyer journey stages

### 1. Qualified problem

**Entry:** ICP fit and credible trigger. **Activities:** quantify current cost, urgency, ownership, and alternative. **Exit:** agreed problem, stakeholders, success outcome, and next technical step. **Artifacts:** discovery notes and mutual action plan.

### 2. Solution fit

Map capabilities to outcomes, integrations, gaps, customer/vendor responsibilities, data flow, and implementation assumptions. Exit only when unsupported needs and required customization are explicit.

### 3. Technical validation

Choose demo, workshop, sandbox, proof of concept, or proof of value based on uncertainty. Define success criteria, dataset, scope, environment, owners, duration, evidence, and stop conditions before work begins.

### 4. Security and compliance

Provide current architecture, data flow, controls, attestations, subprocessor list, DPA, incident/BCP summaries, and shared responsibility. Track questionnaire answers to evidence and expiry. Escalate gaps; never improvise certifications.

### 5. Commercial and procurement

Confirm package, usage assumptions, price, commitment, overage, payment, SLA, support, legal terms, vendor registration, and approvals. Keep negotiated terms connected to enforceable entitlement/billing configuration.

### 6. Implementation and activation

Handoff owners, milestones, dependencies, onboarding workflow, migration, acceptance, training, support, and first-value target. Signed contract is not activation.

### 7. Adoption, renewal, and expansion

Track value realization, usage health, support risk, stakeholder coverage, roadmap fit, renewal timeline, and expansion signals. Conduct evidence-based business reviews.

## Sales motion decision

| Motion | Best fit | Risks | Required assets |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product-led growth | Standard product, low friction, individual/team adoption | Weak enterprise governance or conversion | Sandbox, quickstart, in-product upgrade |
| Sales-assisted | Moderate complexity or larger contract | Handoff friction | Discovery, demo, solution mapping |
| Enterprise field sales | High ACV, security/procurement, multi-team change | Long cycle and high cost | SE, POC, evidence room, mutual plan |
| Partner-led | Ecosystem/integration or geography leverage | Message/control dilution | Certification, deal rules, enablement |

Hybrid motions require explicit routing and ownership so self-service leads are not stranded.

## Proof-of-concept framework

- Business hypothesis and measurable success.
- Scope, exclusions, data, integrations, and environment.
- Customer/vendor owners and availability.
- Baseline and target metrics.
- Security/data handling and deletion.
- Timeline, checkpoints, support, and stop conditions.
- Product gaps versus configurable work.
- Final evidence and conversion decision.

A POC should answer a purchase uncertainty, not provide open-ended free implementation. Do not customize without product/engineering approval and commercial rationale.

## Demo framework

Use a controlled environment and validated data. Tell one persona-specific story: problem, trigger, workflow, outcome, proof. Include expected output and recovery if a dependency fails. Label simulated, preview, or unavailable capabilities. Track demo version with product release.

## Security-review preparation

Maintain an evidence index with document owner, scope, version, issue date, expiry, and NDA classification. Prepare architecture/data-flow diagrams, IAM/encryption/tenant isolation, secure development, vulnerability/penetration results, incident response, continuity/DR, privacy/residency/retention, subprocessors, attestations, and customer responsibilities.

Use an approved answer library. Every questionnaire response should be accurate for the sold deployment model and current date.

## Procurement navigation

Identify legal entity, purchasing process, vendor setup, insurance, tax, accessibility, sanctions/export, data/privacy, security, SLA, support, and signature authority early. Maintain a mutual action plan with owner and target date. Separate non-negotiable controls, standard fallback positions, and executive exceptions.

## Customer success handoff

Required packet:

- Contract/package, entitlement, usage, billing, SLA, and support.
- Stakeholders and desired outcomes.
- Technical architecture, environments, integrations, and data scope.
- Commitments, exceptions, gaps, and roadmap statements.
- Onboarding plan, first-value definition, milestones, and risks.
- Security/privacy obligations and renewal dates.
- Communication cadence and escalation.

Sales, solutions, implementation, success, support, finance, and product should acknowledge the handoff.

## Qualification and discovery prompts

- What event made this problem urgent now?
- What measurable cost or risk exists today?
- Who owns outcome, budget, implementation, security, and approval?
- Which alternatives are being considered, including no action?
- What must be proven technically and commercially?
- What data, systems, regions, and controls are required?
- What is the decision process and deadline?
- What would disqualify the solution?

## Governance and metrics

Track stage conversion, duration, POC success, security/procurement cycle, implementation variance, time to first value, activation, expansion, renewal, gross margin, and reasons lost. Review claim accuracy, customization load, and exception debt. Assign each stage entry/exit, artifact, owner, expected duration, and escalation.

## Launch readiness

- ICP and disqualifiers are validated.
- Packaging maps to enforceable product behavior.
- Demo and POC claims have current evidence.
- Security/legal answer library is reviewed.
- Pricing assumptions reconcile with metering/billing.
- Onboarding and handoffs are tested end to end.
- Sales collateral is versioned with product releases.
- Unsupported requirements and exception authority are explicit.
